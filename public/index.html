👉 Pronouns & Demonstratives (代名詞・指示代名詞)

Master Japanese personal pronouns, plural forms, Ko-so-a-do demonstratives, and essential question words for JLPT N5. Click on any speaker icon 🔊 to hear native audio pronunciation.

1. Personal Pronouns (人称代名詞)

First, Second & Third Person
Japanese Romaji Meaning Formality & Usage Context Audio
私 watashi I / me (neutral/polite) Standard polite pronoun. Used by everyone in polite speech; common casual choice for females.
私 watakushi I / me (very formal) Formal business conversations, official speeches, and formal announcements.
僕 boku I / me (male, casual-polite) Used by males in informal and semi-formal contexts. Modest and polite-casual.
俺 ore I / me (male, rough/casual) Casual male pronoun with friends, family, and peers. Do not use in formal situations.

君 kimi you (casual) Used towards friends, children, or subordinates by males or senior speakers.

彼女 kanojo she / her Refers to a female third person. Can also mean "girlfriend" depending on context.

5. Essential N5 Additions & Key Rules (補足ガイド)

N5 Extra Mastery

🧭 Casual Direction Words (Informal Ko-so-a-do)

  • こっち (kocchi) — this way / here
  • そっち (socchi) — that way / there
  • あっち (acchi) — that way over there
  • どっち (docchi) — which way / which one (of two)

👤 Demonstratives for People (+ Person)

  • この人 / この方 (kono hito / kono kata) — this person
  • その人 / その方 (sono hito / sono kata) — that person
  • あの人 / あの方 (ano hito / ano kata) — that person over there
  • どの人 / どの方 (dono hito / dono kata) — which person

⏱ Duration & Amount Interrogatives

  • どのくらい / どれくらい (dono kurai / dore kurai) — how long / how much duration
  • どのぐらい (dono gurai) — approximate duration / amount

💡 Grammar Rule: これ vs この

• これ / それ / あれ stand ALONE as pronouns: これは本です。 (This is a book.)
• この / その / あの MUST be followed by a noun: この本は面白いです。 (This book is interesting.)

💡 Social Rule: Avoid Overusing "あなた"

In Japanese, calling someone "あなた" (anata) directly can sound distant or overly direct. Always prefer using their name + さん (-san) (e.g. 田中さんは学生ですか) or their title (e.g. 先生).

🧩 Japanese Particles Master Guide (助詞)

Particles (助詞 - joshi) are the glue of Japanese grammar. They follow words to indicate their grammatical role (topic, subject, object, destination, means, time, reason, etc.). Click 🔊 on any example to hear native audio!

1. Essential Case Particles (格助詞)

Core Grammar Foundation

Defines what the sentence is about		 わたしは学生です。 Watashi wa gakusei desu. I am a student.
はwa (ha) Contrastive Marker

4. ⚡ Crucial Particle Comparisons (よく間違える助詞の違い)

JLPT Exam Traps

⚖️ は (wa) vs. が (ga)

• は (Topic): Focuses on what comes AFTER it (the predicate). 田中さんは学生です (As for Tanaka, he is a student).
• が (Subject/Identifier): Focuses on WHO/WHAT comes BEFORE it. 田中さんが来ました (Tanaka is the one who came).
• Question words: Always followed by が: だれが来ましたか？

⚖️ に (ni) vs. で (de)

• に (Existence & Destination): Indicates location of STATIC existence or arrival. 部屋に猫がいる (A cat is in the room) / 学校に行く (Go to school).
• で (Action & Means): Indicates location where an DYNAMIC action takes place. 図書館で勉強する (Study at the library) / バスで行く (Go by bus).

⚖️ に (ni) vs. へ (e)

• に (Destination / Goal Point): Emphasizes the exact arrival destination. 東京に着きます (Arrive in Tokyo).
• へ (Direction): Emphasizes heading toward a direction or journey path. 未来へ進む (Move toward the future).

⚖️ と (to) vs. や (ya)

• と (Exhaustive): Lists EVERY item in the set: りんごとみかん (Apples and oranges — only these two).
• や (Non-exhaustive): Lists representative examples from a larger group: りんごやみかん (など) (Apples, oranges, and other fruits).

⏳ Japanese Tenses & Verb Conjugations (時制・動詞の活用)

Japanese has two main tenses: Non-Past (Present/Future) and Past, divided into Polite (丁寧語) and Plain/Casual (普通形) forms. Master the 3 verb groups and conjugations below! Click 🔊 on any example to hear native audio.

1. The 3 Japanese Verb Groups (動詞の3グループ分類)

Group Classification

🔹 Group 1: 五段動詞 (Godan / U-Verbs)

Verbs ending in -u, -ku, -gu, -su, -tsu, -nu, -bu, -mu, or -ru (preceded by a/u/o vowels).

Conjugation Rule: Change the ending -u vowel to -i + ます.

  • 書く (kaku) → 書きます (kakimasu - write)
  • 飲む (nomu) → 飲みます (nomimasu - drink)
  • 話す (hanasu) → 話します (hanashimasu - speak)
  • 待つ (matsu) → 待ちます (machimasu - wait)
  • 行く (iku) → 行きます (ikimasu - go)

🔹 Group 2: 一段動詞 (Ichidan / Ru-Verbs)

Verbs ending in -iru or -eru (preceded by i/e vowels).

Conjugation Rule: Drop る (ru) and add ます, ない, or て directly.

  • 食べる (taberu) → 食べます (tabemasu - eat)
  • 見る (miru) → 見ます (mimasu - see/watch)
  • 起きる (okiru) → 起きます (okimasu - wake up)
  • 寝る (neru) → 寝ます (nemasu - sleep)
  • 教える (oshieru) → 教えます (oshiemasu - teach)

🔹 Group 3: 不規則動詞 (Irregular Verbs)

Only two irregular verbs exist in Japanese! Memorize them directly:

  • する (suru - to do):
    → します (shimasu - present polite)
    → しない (shinai - plain neg) / した (shita - plain past)
    → して (shite - te-form)
  • 来る (kuru - to come):
    → 来ます (kimasu - present polite)
    → 来ない (konai - plain neg) / 来た (kita - plain past)
    → 来て (kite - te-form)

3. Te-Form (〜て形) Master Cheat Sheet & Song Rules

Essential Conjugation Key

The て-form is the most critical conjugation in Japanese — used for requests (〜てください), continuous tense (〜ている), permission (〜てもいい), prohibition (〜てはいけない), and connecting sequential actions!

1. う・つ・る (u, tsu, ru)
→ って (-tte)
• 買う (kau) → 買って (katte)
• 待つ (matsu) → 待って (matte)
• 帰る (kaeru) → 帰って (kaette)
2. む・ぶ・ぬ (mu, bu, nu)
→ んで (-nde)
• 飲む (nomu) → 飲んで (nonde)
• 遊ぶ (asobu) → 遊んで (asonde)
• 死ぬ (shinu) → 死んで (shinde)
3. く (ku) / ぐ (gu)
→ いて (-ite) / いで (-ide)
• 書く (kaku) → 書いて (kaite)
• 泳ぐ (oyogu) → 泳いで (oyoide)
⚠️ Exception: 行く → 行って (itte)
4. す (su)
→ して (-shite)
• 話す (hanasu) → 話して (hanashite)
• 出す (dasu) → 出して (dashite)
• 消す (kesu) → 消して (keshite)
5. 一段動詞 (Ichidan / Ru)
Drop る → て (-te)
• 食べる (taberu) → 食べて (tabete)
• 見る (miru) → 見て (mite)
• 起きる (okiru) → 起きて (okite)
6. 不規則動詞 (Irregular)
Special Conjugation
• する (suru) → して (shite)
• 来る (kuru) → 来て (kite)
